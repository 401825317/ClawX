title: ACP Chat State And History Authority
type: ai-coding-rule

Main owns ACP process, SDK, routing lifecycle, and serialization of operations on the shared ACP connection; Renderer owns semantic reduction into an in-memory timeline. One session-scoped Renderer coordinator owns each exact `sessionKey + generation` snapshot and advances a monotonic revision for its commits. Notifications emitted during `session/load` are returned as one generation-scoped raw batch and reduced in one Renderer state commit. Renderer may temporarily buffer matching host events during the IPC result handoff, while ordinary live prompt updates continue through host events in receive order. The first live text chunk is published immediately; only adjacent pure-text chunks for the same session, generation, role, and message may share a publication window of at most `32 ms`. A tool, permission, media, completion, terminal, send, cancel, or navigation boundary flushes buffered text first.

A pending prompt may retain a bounded Main routing context and Renderer timeline snapshot so navigation cannot drop its stream. An accepted asynchronous image, video, or explicit `MEDIA:` delivery retains its original session and Turn owner until it reaches a visible terminal state; visiting another conversation or starting a later prompt does not transfer or cancel that ownership. Returning through ordinary ACP replay builds a fresh authoritative history base and may carry forward only explicitly marked, bounded Renderer compatibility media from the owned snapshot, including a generic proven video-failure projection. Ordinary text, tools, thoughts, plans, permissions, and ordering remain replay-owned, and attachment references must be rebound to the new exact session generation. These contexts remain memory-only; Renderer keeps at most three settled, unconsumed background snapshots and three unretained coordinator records, while explicitly retained in-flight owners are exempt until settlement. Large retained timelines are released after consumption. Permission requests are interactive only for an active prompt. Stale generations of the same session are ignored, and ClawX does not persist a second ACP ledger or reduced Chat history.

ACP replay is the primary history authority. The only approved transcript-derived content supplements are best-effort recovery of asynchronous image-generation completions with proven `image_generate` context, recovery of explicit line-leading assistant OpenClaw `MEDIA:` attachment directives omitted by ACP, and prefix-preserving repair of the sole existing Assistant segment in the exact current Turn after ACP explicitly reports prompt failure and the transcript Assistant record is also marked failed. The general attachment exception does not require image-generation context, but it recovers only attachment references. Failed-Turn repair performs one read, cannot create a segment, and cannot replace divergent text. A separate metadata-only supplement may annotate an ACP-replayed assistant turn with whole-turn duration because ACP `session/load` omits the original event timestamps; it cannot create turns or content. These exceptions remain marked and in memory; do not generalize them to bare paths, surrounding transcript prose, ordinary messages, tool cards, plans, permissions, thoughts, file activity, or any parallel history.

Historical transcript reads are limited to the newest `1000` message records. A successful live prompt reads content immediately and begins its bounded retry schedule after `1500 ms`; retries for one Turn execute serially and a later attempt cannot invalidate an older attempt that is still resolving. General attachment and timing alignment treat history as a suffix and match the binary-free OpenClaw prompt-text projection of structured ACP user blocks by duplicate occurrence from the tail; they must not parse or globally remove user-authored resource marker text. Attachment-only empty projections remain eligible, and live content alignment also requires the exact optimistic user identity. Supplement operations are keyed by session, generation, and live user message, so unrelated Turns and conversations remain independent. Every asynchronous result must retain that owner identity and its own operation attempt. Unmatched, ambiguous, superseded-generation, or stale-operation work cannot mutate the timeline or timing annotations; navigation alone is not staleness.
